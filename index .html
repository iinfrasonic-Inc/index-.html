Cross-instrumental program

Don't just play an instrument.
Become a musician.

Music is never one instrument. It's rhythm, harmony, melody and voice holding each other up. So every Infrasonic student works beyond their primary instrument from early on — not as an upgrade or an add-on, but because that is what becoming a musician actually involves.

You hear the whole song

Sit behind a kit once and you stop counting bars — you start feeling them. Every instrument you touch changes what you can hear in all the others.

You can take any seat

A musician who can hold a rhythm, find a chord and carry a line is useful in any room — never the one waiting to be handed their part.

You play with people, not beside them

Knowing what everyone else is doing is what turns a group of students into a band — the difference between playing together and playing at the same time.

The four parts every student gets their hands on
Rhythm
Drums & percussion

The pulse everything else is built on. Play it once and your timing stops being something you have to think about.

Harmony
Keys & guitar

The chords that give a song its colour and mood — and the quickest way to understand why music moves you the way it does.

Melody
Violin, lead & keys

The line people hum on the way home. Carrying it teaches phrasing, tone and how to hold a room's attention.

Voice
Vocals

The instrument you already own. Using it sharpens your ear faster than anything else you can pick up.

How it runs through your journey
Built in from the start

Time beyond your primary instrument is part of your path from early on — not something unlocked once you're advanced enough to have earned it.

Hands on the instrument

You sit at it and play it. Watching a demonstration teaches you about an instrument; playing one teaches you music.

Put to work on stage

It comes together in ensemble and performance, where knowing the other parts is what lets a group play as one thing instead of four.

A complete musician doesn't just know their part. They know where it fits.
